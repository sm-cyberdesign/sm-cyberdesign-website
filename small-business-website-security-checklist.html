How to Secure a Small Business Website: A Practical Checklist

Small businesses benefit from straightforward technology practices that are easy to understand and maintain. This guide focuses on useful steps, not complicated terminology.

In this guide
  1. Start with the essentials
  2. Protect the accounts that control your site
  3. Keep software and integrations current
  4. Review forms and customer information
  5. Make backups you can restore
  6. Plan for maintenance and incidents

1. Start with the essentials

A secure website does not need to be complicated. Start by identifying who manages the site, where it is hosted, and which accounts control its domain, hosting, and content.

2. Protect the accounts that control your site

Use unique passwords and multifactor authentication where available. Limit administrator access to people who need it and remove unused accounts promptly.

3. Keep software and integrations current

Maintain your content management system, plugins, themes, and connected services. Test updates on a staging environment when possible, and remove unused integrations.

4. Review forms and customer information

Only request information you need. Verify that forms use HTTPS, reach the correct recipient, and do not expose submissions in public dashboards or logs.

5. Make backups you can restore

Know who creates backups, how frequently they run, where they are stored, and whether restoration has been tested. A backup is only useful when it can be recovered.

6. Plan for maintenance and incidents

Document a contact person, renewal dates, and what to do if the site is compromised or unavailable. Review your checklist after significant changes.
